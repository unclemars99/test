# D000d — Generic SSL gives partial but unstable cross-domain gains

## Experiment

E000-C: feature-level denoising autoencoder (DAE) vs train-only PCA95 under 3-fold leave-one-cutter-out evaluation on PHM2010-derived features.

## Main evidence

Aggregate:

- DAE12 mean wear MAE: 22.08 vs PCA95: 22.73;
- DAE12 mean wear R2: 0.231 vs PCA95: 0.194;
- DAE12 worst-fold R2: -0.604 vs PCA95: -0.835;
- DAE12 fold R2 range: 1.375 vs PCA95: 1.614;
- DAE12 cutter silhouette: 0.104 vs PCA95: 0.119.

Fold behavior:

- held-out C1: DAE mean R2 0.525 vs PCA 0.639;
- held-out C4: DAE mean R2 0.771 vs PCA 0.779;
- held-out C6: DAE mean R2 -0.604 vs PCA -0.835.

DAE therefore mainly reduces the severity of the hardest-domain collapse rather than uniformly improving every domain.

## Stability warning

C6 DAE R2 across five seeds ranges from -1.553 to +0.222. This is a large instability and prevents a strong claim that generic SSL has solved cross-domain representation.

The DAE also compresses both state and domain cosine distances. Its sharedness ratio is slightly higher than PCA because domain distance shrinks more strongly; this ratio must not be interpreted alone.

## Decision

**H001: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED on this public feature-level benchmark.**

Evidence supports the narrower claim that nonlinear self-supervised compression can modestly improve average and worst-domain transfer relative to PCA, but it does not yet produce a stable shared representation across all cutters.

H001 is not considered fully supported because:

1. C6 remains a failure on average;
2. random-seed variance is large;
3. the experiment uses engineered feature vectors, not raw process signals;
4. PHM2010 wear labels have known limitations;
5. H002 Process Token is not tested here.

## Next

Test whether a generic invariance-oriented SSL objective can reduce seed/domain instability before introducing physical Process Token structure. The next generic baseline should focus on held-out C6 robustness and worst-fold consistency, not only mean R2.
